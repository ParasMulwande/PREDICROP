SOIL INTELLIGENCE

Understanding Your Soil

Soil testing helps farmers understand nutrient availability, soil condition, and the factors that influence crop growth and yield.

KEY NUTRIENTS

What the Soil Contains

🌿 Nitrogen (N)

Nitrogen is essential for leaf and stem growth. It is a key component of chlorophyll and amino acids. Deficiency causes yellowing of older leaves and stunted growth. Excess can cause excessive vegetative growth at the expense of fruiting.

🌱 Phosphorous (P)

Phosphorus supports root development, flowering, and seed production. It is critical for energy transfer and photosynthesis. Deficiency often causes purpling of leaves and weak root systems. Most available in slightly acidic to neutral soils.

💧 Potassium (K)

Potassium regulates water uptake, disease resistance, and overall crop quality. It activates enzymes and controls stomata. Deficient crops show scorched leaf margins and poor fruit development. Critical for drought tolerance.

⚗️ Soil pH

Soil pH determines nutrient availability and microbial activity. Most crops grow best between pH 6.0–7.0. Acidic soils (below 5.5) lock up phosphorus; alkaline soils (above 7.5) can cause iron and zinc deficiency. Test before amending.

SOIL TESTING

What Is Soil Testing?

About Soil Testing

Soil testing is a process of determining the chemical, physical and biological properties of soil. It is used to determine the suitability of soil for different agricultural applications, such as crop growing and food production.

First, the soil sample is collected, weighed, and then placed in a container to preserve the moisture content. The sample is then taken to a laboratory where it is analyzed for pH, nitrogen, and phosphorus levels, among other things.

The data collected from soil tests are used to determine how much fertilizer should be added to an area to ensure proper nutrient levels are maintained throughout the growing season.

How To Get Your Soil Tested

01

DIY Testing Kits

Test your soil at home with specialized soil testing kits available at agricultural stores. Quick, affordable, and suitable for basic N-P-K and pH checks.

02

Professional Laboratory Testing

Send soil samples to a state or private agricultural laboratory for comprehensive analysis. Provides detailed reports including micronutrients, organic matter, and amendment recommendations.

03

Government Soil Health Card Scheme

The Indian government's Soil Health Card (SHC) scheme provides free soil testing through Krishi Vigyan Kendras and state agricultural departments. Every farmer is eligible.

What Does a Soil Test Tell You?

The goal of soil testing is to determine nutrient balances so they can be adjusted to support healthy plant growth. Soil tests can help identify nutrients that are missing from the soil and guide the addition of fertilizer or compost.

Soil tests can also identify excess nutrients and provide information about pH levels, helping determine whether plants need more acidic or alkaline conditions. One of the most common tests measures nitrogen (N), phosphorus (P), and potassium (K) — commonly referred to as NPK tests.

Does soil need to be dry for testing?

A soil sample generally needs to be slightly moist rather than completely dry. It is recommended to collect the sample when the soil is at field capacity — meaning it holds together when squeezed but is not overly saturated. This allows better mixing and analysis of the soil sample.
